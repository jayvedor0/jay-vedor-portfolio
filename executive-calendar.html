How I handled it

  1. 1

    Double-booking on Tuesday

    A vendor demo landed on top of a client consultation at 2:00 PM ET. The client meeting stayed; I moved the demo to Thursday 10:00 AM and confirmed the new time with the vendor.

  2. 2

    Request into a focus block

    A partner asked for Wednesday at 9:00 AM ET, inside the protected focus block. I offered 10:00 AM or 3:00 PM instead; they took 10:00 AM.

  3. 3

    Candidate asked to reschedule

    A candidate couldn’t make Thursday. I moved the interview to Friday at 11:30 AM ET, after stand-up, and sent an updated invite to the candidate and the hiring team.

Calendar rules

Focus blocks (Mon, Wed, Fri, 9–10 AM ET) stay protected. External meetings go between 10 AM and 5 PM ET.

Every change gets an updated invite and a short confirmation to everyone involved.

Reply templates

  • Reschedule a meeting

    Hi [Name], [Executive] has a conflict at the original time. Would [Option 1] or [Option 2] (ET) work for you instead? I’ll send an updated invite as soon as you confirm.

  • Protect a focus block

    Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out. [Executive]’s morning is blocked for focused work, but [Option 1] or [Option 2] (ET) are open. Let me know which suits you best.

  • Follow up on a pending reply

    Hi [Name], just following up on [topic] from [day]. Is there anything you need from us to move this forward? Happy to help.

  1. Protect the executive’s time

    Focus blocks and meeting hours are rules, not suggestions, so requests get alternatives instead of a yes by default.

  2. Every email has a home

    Each message is labeled reply today, delegated, waiting, or filed, so the inbox shows only what still needs a decision.

  3. Work across time zones

    Eastern and Philippine time sit side by side, so U.S. hours are covered without guesswork.
