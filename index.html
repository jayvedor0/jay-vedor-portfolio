The problem
In a busy family-law practice, trust balances, unbilled work, open invoices, and upcoming events live in different places. That makes it easy for a replenishment request to go out late.
My role
At a U.S. divorce law firm, I issued invoices, monitored client trust accounts, and followed up on replenishments, balances, and required payments. This sample rebuilds that workflow with fictional data.
What I handled
Invoicing, trust-account monitoring, replenishment and balance follow-ups, payment conversations with clients, and billing updates with the legal team.
Process
  1. Log each client’s balance, open billing, and next event
  2. Compare the balance with the required trust
  3. Calculate the amount needed by formula
  4. Set status and priority
  5. Record the next action and follow up
Tools
Excel formulas (MAX, COUNTIF), consistent status and priority rules, and a web version that runs the same formula.
Outcome
One sheet answers three questions: who needs attention, how much is needed, and what happens next. In the sample data it flags 6 of 20 accounts and totals $43,142 to request, all calculated rather than typed in.

How I work

Capture it. Triage it. Close the loop.

A simple operating rhythm for work that arrives from five different places and still needs one clear owner.

  1. Capture

    Collect requests, messages, deadlines, dependencies, and loose ends.

  2. Triage

    Decide urgency, ownership, dependencies, and the next action.

  3. Execute

    Handle the task, communicate with the right people, and update the system.

  4. Close the loop

    Document what happened and make sure nothing is left hanging.
